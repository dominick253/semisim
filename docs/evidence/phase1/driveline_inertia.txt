## The change (D53)

While the clutch is engaged, spinning the drive wheels up also spins the
flywheel and the driveline up through the gearbox, so the saturated-branch
integrator works against the wheel inertia plus the engine-side inertia
reflected through the square of the total driveline ratio:

  I_effective = WHEEL_INERTIA_KG_M2
                + (FLYWHEEL_INERTIA_KG_M2 + DRIVELINE_INERTIA_KG_M2)
                * pow(ratio * FINAL_DRIVE_RATIO, 2)
  gear 1: 2.0 + 1.7 * (4.834 * 3.70)^2 = 2.0 + 1.7 * 319.91 = 545.8 kg m^2
          (against 2.0 pre-slice)
  gear 5: 2.0 + 1.7 * (1.000 * 3.70)^2 = 25.3 kg m^2

In gear 1 the redline (1800 rpm) maps to 10.539 rad/s of wheel and the
shutdown (1900) to 11.124 rad/s, so the fuel cut now bites BEFORE the
shutdown. Pinned decisions from the brief: applied ONLY in the saturated-branch
integrator (the gripping branch keeps the AS4d no-slip assignment, so dry stays
byte-stable); transmission.ratio() is computed once per physics tick, outside
the per-wheel loop, and passed as a plain number (no String/Array/Dictionary
per tick); no new constant, no fudge factor, no damping, no clamp; the 200
rad/s numerical clamp stays where it is.

## What is still not modelled

- No torque converter and no clutch slip: the engaged-clutch path is a rigid
  coupling (D51). The reflected inertia assumes the engine-side mass spins at
  exactly ratio x the wheel rate at every tick; a real clutch slips and stores
  and releases energy. Here the flywheel and driveline are a fixed reflected
  load on the wheel's integrator, not a second state.
- A single drive-axle omega scalar: one shared wheel_omega_rad_s for both drive
  wheels (unchanged by this slice).
- The 1900 rpm shutdown stays reachable in principle (engine_rpm clamps at
  1900; a wheel driven externally past 11.124 rad/s in gear 1 reads the
  shutdown and the engine dies with no restart path, as before). From every
  regime exercised by the suite and the probes it is no longer reachable: the
  1800 fuel cut bites first.
