CITYHAUL AS4f: the saturated branch uses the spec's longitudinal mu-slip curve, and ABS releases
===============================================================================================

Repo state: on main at HEAD 95ab9f3 (after AS4e). This slice changes only
scripts/vehicles/combination_controller.gd (the saturated branch and the ABS
release move), tests/unit/test_tire_slip_curve.gd (new), this file, and one row
(D50) in docs/DEVIATIONS.md. No PHYSICS_CONSTANTS value, the tire model, the
friction ellipse, the ABS thresholds, the AS4e surface channel, or the AS4d
no-slip assignment were touched.

## The two measured defects

1. The spec's longitudinal curve was dead code. scripts/physics/tire_model.gd
   provides TireModel.longitudinal_force(kappa, fz, mu, braking), the Pacejka
   formula with the spec-pinned cornering stiffness (TIRE_BX_DRIVE 12.0,
   TIRE_BX_BRAKE 14.0, section 5.2). `grep -rn "longitudinal_force" scripts/`
   found the definition and no caller. The saturated branch instead capped the
   delivered force at adhesion = mu*fz, a constant with no slip dependence. On
   ice (4 s full throttle) the drive omega ran to the 200 rad/s integrator
   clamp and stayed there:

       SAT t=0.0 speed=1.69 omega=200.000 v_over_r=3.010 slip=+65.4483 abs=true
             adhesion_n=14389 demand_n=21314

   A real spinning tyre moves down the mu-slip curve past its peak, so the
   transmitted force falls as the wheel spins up and the spin is
   self-limiting. With a flat cap there is no counter-force.
2. The ABS release check lived inside the saturated branch, so once engaged it
   stayed engaged: on ice the probe reported abs=true at every sample (t=1.0
   through t=6.0) after the vehicle had stopped and slip was about 0.0000.

## The change (scripts/vehicles/combination_controller.gd, _step_wheel)

1. Saturated branch (absf(demand_n) > adhesion): the delivered force is now
   TireModel.longitudinal_force(w.slip_ratio, fz, w.mu(), braking), where
   braking = (demand_n * v_forward_wheel < 0.0) -- the demand opposes the
   wheel's direction of travel (a drive demand and forward travel are same-sign,
   a brake demand opposes them; at rest both are ~0 so the predicate is false,
   i.e. the drive column). No clamp is applied on top: the curve is bounded by
   its own peak (see the parent-review section). The old `adhesion * 0.85` ABS
   modulation is dropped (D50): the curve's fall past its peak now does that
   job physically.
2. The ABS release check moved out of the saturated branch so it runs every
   tick for a grounded wheel: abs_engaged clears when
   absf(w.slip_ratio) < ABS_RELEASE_SLIP. The engage condition stays where it
   was (inside the saturated branch).

     0.1483 (brake) and cannot exceed it, so delivered_n never passes adhesion.

     with a print and counted fires: 0 in the ice saturation probe, 0 in the dry
     saturation probe, 0 in the spin probe, 0 in probe_yaw_push, and 0 across the
     whole 196-test unit suite. Unreachable, and where it could fire (a wheel
     still spinning up under brake torque) the curve's sign is the physically
     correct force direction, so the guard would have suppressed a real transient
     force. Both removals left the spin, saturation and suite numbers unchanged
     within the run-to-run tick drift, which is the expected result for dead code.
3. Corrected the brief's pinned curve constants, which the child's measurement
   exposed as wrong: analytic peak kappa is tan(pi / (2 * TIRE_CX)) / Bx =
   0.17304 (drive) and 0.14832 (brake), not 0.1709 / 0.1465, and the curve at
   kappa 1.0 is 0.87181 / 0.86357 of peak, not 0.8674 / 0.8598. The child's

## Measurement pitfall

The SAT probe's brake-phase samples (t=1.0+) have run-to-run tick drift: two
runs of the identical original code differ at t=2.0 (omega -0.037 vs +0.010,
normal_n 57964 vs 58763), so the once-per-second brake samples are not a
byte-comparison point. The dry LAUNCH is the reliable dry comparison (spin
probe above, matches AS4e within 0.3 percent), and the SAT t=0.0 (brake start)
is the hard comparison point (byte-identical to AS4e on the original code).
