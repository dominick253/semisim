## The AS4g change (pre-existing in the working tree, documented here for the record)

`_engine_rpm_from_wheel_rate(w)` returns `w.omega * RATIO` where RATIO is the rpm
per rad/s of wheel spin in the current gear (`60.0 / TAU * ratio * FINAL_DRIVE_RATIO`);
`engine_rpm` is `max(engine_rpm, _engine_rpm_from_wheel_rate(w))` after each wheel's
physics step. The engaged-clutch path has no torque converter modelled, so the
driveline is a rigid coupling and the engine must follow whichever side of it spins
faster (D51). Both the road-speed term and the wheel-rate term pass through the same
idle clamp (floor 700) and the same 1900 rpm shutdown clamp that already gated the
road-speed term.

AS4g measured outcome: the pre-AS4f ice launch pinned the drive omega at the 200
rad/s integrator clamp with slip +69.0761; AS4g bounded the spin to 22.860 rad/s
on a one-tick startup transient with peak slip +0.2373 (numbers below). Dry is
byte-identical (the wheel never outruns the road, so the max() is a no-op).

Redline dead constant: PHYSICS_CONSTANTS.ENGINE_REDLINE_RPM is 1800 and
ENGINE_SHUTDOWN_RPM is 1900, and the torque curve is flat 1600 Nm across the whole
1800-1900 band (same 1600 Nm as the 1700-1800 band; 1450 Nm at 2000 rpm). Nothing
in the drivetrain read the redline, so wheelspin in a low gear ran the engine
straight through it into the 1900 shutdown, where `engine_running` goes false --
and there is no restart path (`air.restart_engine()` is never called). Same defect
family as the once-unused mu_wet/mu_ice columns.

## The change (scripts/vehicles/combination_controller.gd, _drive_wheel_torque)

  # AS4h rev limiter: fuel cut at the pinned redline (1800). The curve is flat
  # 1800-1900, so without it wheelspin drags the engine into the 1900 shutdown.
  # Gated on throttle > 0.0 (engine braking untouched); no hysteresis, no constant.
  if throttle > 0.0 and engine_rpm >= PHYSICS_CONSTANTS.ENGINE_REDLINE_RPM:
      return 0.0  # rev limiter: fuel cut at the pinned redline

Gated on `throttle > 0.0` so the closed-throttle engine-braking branch (the
negative-torque return earlier in the function) is untouched. No new constant, no
hysteresis state: the cut re-arms the tick the rpm falls back below the redline,
and the wheel slowing down under zero torque provides the separation.

## What this slice does and does not fix

The fuel cut makes the pinned redline a mechanical boundary: under throttle the
engine holds 1800-1802 rpm instead of running through the flat 1600 Nm band into
the 1900 shutdown, and the pinned shutdown stays reachable only by external
driveline drive (the one-tick startup transient). What it does not fix, stated
plainly: a from-rest launch on ice in gear 1 still stalls the engine (dead on
tick 2, no restart path, combination crawls at ~0.04 m/s). The startup transient
outruns the cut -- the wheel spike is one tick and the shutdown clamps before the
cut can engage -- and there is no restart path by design of this slice. That is
the residual: the combination can still kill its engine if the driver floors it
in a low gear on a low-grip surface; the limiter keeps every case the driver
reaches while moving pinned at the redline instead of dead.
